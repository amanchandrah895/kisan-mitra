# Major diseases of tomato

## Damping off (Pythium aphanidermatum)
Seedlings rot before emergence, or after emergence the stem becomes soft and water-soaked and seedlings topple over. It is favoured by high humidity and soil moisture, cloudy weather, temperatures below 24 C, crowded seedlings and poor drainage. Raise the nursery on raised beds 15 cm high and avoid waterlogging. Treat seed with Trichoderma asperellum 4 g per kg or Bacillus subtilis 10 g per kg 24 hours before sowing. Drench with copper oxychloride 50% WP at 2.5 g per litre at 4 litres per square metre. Source: https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_1.html

## Early blight (Alternaria solani)
Leaves show brown spots with concentric rings (a bull's-eye pattern) and yellow margins; fruits get infected near the calyx with brown concentric rings. It is favoured by warm, humid weather (24-29 C) and rain. The fungus survives in crop debris and on seed, and spreads by rain and wind. Remove and destroy crop debris, rotate crops and treat seed with Trichoderma asperellum 4 g per kg. Sprays include azoxystrobin 23% SC 200 ml per acre, mancozeb 35% SC 1 kg per acre or zineb 75% WP 800 g per acre. Source: https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_2.html

## Late blight (Phytophthora infestans)
Water-soaked black lesions appear on leaves and stems and expand quickly; white growth may appear on leaves, and fruits get dark brown lesions with soft rot. It is favoured by cool nights, warm days and long wet periods from rain or fog; the optimum temperature for sporulation is 18-22 C. Provide proper drainage and rotate crops. Apply Trichoderma asperellum to soil at 1.0 kg per acre. Sprays include cyazofamid 34.5% SC 80 ml per acre or azoxystrobin 23% SC 200 ml per acre. Source: https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_8.html

## Fusarium wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici)
Lower leaves turn yellow, petioles droop, the vascular tissue turns brown and the plant wilts. It is favoured by dry weather, high soil temperature and low soil moisture. Use soil solarisation, crop rotation and removal of infected plants; dip seedlings in Bacillus subtilis 5 g per litre and apply B. subtilis 1 kg with 50 kg farmyard manure per acre 30 days after transplanting. Source: https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_3.html

## Bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)
Plants wilt rapidly and completely and a white bacterial ooze comes out of cut stems. It is favoured by high soil moisture. Use crop rotation and disease-free seedlings, restrict irrigation, drench bleaching powder at 4 kg per acre and apply neem cake at 100 kg per acre. Source: https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_5.html

## Leaf curl (Tomato leaf curl virus)
Plants are severely stunted, leaves roll downward and crinkle, older leaves become leathery and plants look bushy. The virus is spread mainly by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci. Raise the nursery under protection (insect-proof net), grow sorghum, maize or bajra as barrier crops, use disease-free seedlings, install yellow sticky traps at 5 per acre and remove the weed host Abutilon indicum. To control whitefly, spray one of dimethoate 30 EC 200 ml per acre, thiamethoxam 25 WG 200 g per acre or imidacloprid 17.8 SL 200 ml per acre.
