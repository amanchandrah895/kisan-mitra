# Vermicompost production and use

## What it is
Vermicomposting uses earthworms to convert organic waste into worm casts, a fine, granular and stable compost sometimes called "black gold". Mixing legume and non-legume residues improves its quality.

## Earthworm species
Eisenia foetida (red earthworm) is preferred because it multiplies quickly; it is a surface feeder and converts material into compost in 45 to 50 days. Eudrilus eugeniae (night crawler) and Perionyx excavatus are also used. E. foetida is 3-10 cm long, matures in 50-55 days and produces a cocoon every 3 days.

## Bed method (preferred)
Make beds of 6 x 2 x 2 feet on a pucca or kachcha floor in a cool, moist and shaded place. The pit method (5 x 5 x 3 feet cement pits) is not preferred because of poor aeration and waterlogging.

## Step-by-step procedure
1. Mix cow dung and chopped dry leaves in a 3:1 ratio and let it partly decompose for 15 to 20 days.
2. Lay a 15-20 cm layer of chopped dry leaves or grass at the bottom of the bed.
3. Fill the bed (6 x 2 x 2 feet) with 1.5 to 2.0 quintals of partly decomposed material.
4. Release 1,500 to 2,000 red earthworms on the upper layer.
5. Sprinkle water immediately and keep the bed moist by daily sprinkling; cover with gunny bags or polythene.
6. Turn the material once after 30 days for aeration.
7. The compost is ready in 45 to 50 days; output is about three-quarters of the raw material weight.

## Harvesting
Stop watering when the compost is ready. Heap it on partly decomposed cow dung so the worms move into the dung; after two days separate and sieve the compost.

## Precautions
Keep the floor compact so worms do not escape. Use cow dung that is 15-20 days old to avoid heat. Keep plastics, chemicals, pesticides and metals out. Maintain moisture at 30-40% and temperature at 18-25 C.

## Nutrient content
Typical analysis: total nitrogen 1.02%, available phosphorus 0.30%, available potassium 0.24%, organic carbon 11.88%, pH 6.8. Nutrients are released within a month of application.

## Application rates
- Field crops: 5 to 6 tonnes per hectare.
- Fruit trees: 3 to 5 kg per plant in the tree basin.
- Potted plants: 100 to 200 g per pot.
Use vermicompost as one part of integrated nutrient management.
