# Rice main-field nutrient management

## Soil-test based application
Nutrients should be applied according to soil test results. Nitrogen can be managed with the Leaf Colour Chart (LCC). Phosphorus and potassium can be managed through site-specific nutrient management using the omission plot technique.

## Blanket fertilizer recommendations (kg N, P2O5, K2O per hectare)
When soil-test based recommendations cannot be followed, the blanket doses are:
- Short-duration varieties in the dry season: 150:50:50 in the Cauvery delta and Coimbatore tract, and 120:40:40 in other tracts.
- Medium and long-duration varieties in the wet season: 150:50:50.
- Hybrid rice: 175:60:60.
- Low-nitrogen-responsive cultivars such as Improved White Ponni: 75:50:50. For Ponni, nitrogen is given in three splits at active tillering, panicle initiation and heading, along with green leaf manure or farmyard manure.

## Split application of N and K
Apply phosphorus fully as basal and incorporate it into the soil. Nitrogen and potassium are split into four equal doses applied at basal, active tillering, panicle initiation and heading. Tillering and panicle initiation are crucial stages, and the recommended quantity should not be reduced at these stages.

## Crop stages (days after sowing)
- Short duration (105 days): active tillering 35-40, panicle initiation 45-50, heading 70-75.
- Medium duration (135 days): active tillering 50-55, panicle initiation 70-75, heading 100-105.
- Long duration (150 days): active tillering 55-60, panicle initiation 85-90, heading 115-120.

## Rock phosphate
With green manure, rock phosphate is a cheap source of phosphorus, and the following rice crop then does not need phosphorus. Rock phosphate mixed with single super phosphate or DAP in 75:25 or 50:50 proportion is as effective as SSP or DAP alone.

## Leaf Colour Chart (LCC) for nitrogen timing
LCC readings start at 14 days after transplanting (or 21 days after sowing for direct-seeded rice) and are repeated weekly up to heading. Use the youngest fully opened leaf (third leaf from the top), read in the morning between 8 and 10 am at 10 places in the field. The critical LCC value is 3.0 for low-nitrogen-responsive cultures such as White Ponni and 4.0 for other varieties and hybrids. If 6 out of 10 readings are below the critical value, apply 35 kg N per hectare in the dry season or 30 kg N per hectare in the wet season.
