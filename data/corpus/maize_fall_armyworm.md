# Fall armyworm in maize

## About the pest
Fall armyworm (FAW, Spodoptera frugiperda) is a polyphagous pest that attacks maize and many other crops. In 2024 PPQS reported incidence above the economic threshold level in parts of Maharashtra (Nagpur, Amravati and Nashik districts). Favourable weather and continuous availability of maize favour the pest, so early action is advised.

## Cultural and mechanical control
- Practise clean cultivation and remove alternate host plants to break the life cycle.
- Hand-pick egg masses and larvae and destroy them by crushing or dipping them in water.
- Deep summer ploughing before sowing exposes pupae to predators.
- Intercrop maize with pulses such as pigeon pea, black gram or green gram.
- Erect bird perches in the field during the early crop stage (up to 30 days) because birds feed on the larvae.

## Trap crop and botanicals
Sow 3 to 4 rows of a trap crop such as Napier grass around the maize field. When the trap crop shows FAW damage, spray 5% neem seed kernel extract (NSKE) or azadirachtin 1500 ppm.

## Pheromone traps
Install pheromone traps in the field for mass trapping of male moths.

## Biological control
Release the egg parasitoids Trichogramma pretiosum and Telenomus remus at 50,000 per acre at weekly intervals. Avoid chemical sprays for a few days around each release so that the parasitoids survive.

## Chemical control
Use only insecticides and biopesticides that have a label claim for fall armyworm on maize approved by the Central Insecticides Board and Registration Committee. PPQS publishes the list of recommended insecticides and biopesticides for FAW.

## Surveillance and reporting
Extension staff should monitor the pest through the National Pest Surveillance System (NPSS). Pest incidence can be reported to the Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage.
