# Soil Health Card scheme

## Launch and purpose
The Soil Health Card scheme was launched by the Prime Minister on 19 February 2015 at Suratgarh, Rajasthan, with the slogan "Swasth Dharaa. Khet Haraa." (Healthy Earth, Green Farm). It helps state governments issue soil health cards to all farmers. Each card gives the nutrient status of the farm's soil and recommends the dose of fertilizers and soil amendments needed.

## Parameters tested (12)
The soil sample is analysed for 12 parameters:
- Macronutrients: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K);
- Secondary nutrient: sulphur (S);
- Micronutrients: zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), copper (Cu), manganese (Mn), boron (B);
- Physical parameters: pH, electrical conductivity (EC) and organic carbon (OC).
Based on these results the card advises which fertilizers to use and in what quantity.

## How a farmer gets the card
Soil samples are collected from the farm by the state agriculture department and tested in soil testing laboratories. A printed card is given for each holding. The scheme's objectives mention issuing cards every two years, while the factsheet's key features mention once every three years, so the farmer should check the current cycle with the local agriculture office. Details are available on the portal soilhealth.dac.gov.in.

## Soil testing labs by youth and farmer groups
Village youth and farmers up to 40 years of age, self-help groups, farmer producer companies and cooperatives can set up soil health labs. A lab costs up to Rs 5 lakh, of which up to 75% can be funded by the Centre and states.

## Impact
A National Productivity Council study (2017) found that chemical fertilizer use fell by 8-10% and crop yields rose by 5-6% after farmers followed soil health card recommendations. About 10.7 crore cards were issued in cycle I (2015-17) and 11.97 crore in cycle II (2017-19).
